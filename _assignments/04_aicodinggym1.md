## Overview

In this challenge, you will work on a real machine learning competition from the [AI Coding Gym](https://aicodinggym.com/) platform where frontier AI agents fall short. Your goal is to **outperform the AI baseline** by applying your own domain judgment, iterative experimentation, and strategic use of AI as a collaborative tool.

This is a **Bronze-tier** challenge: AI gets close but not quite. You can win with careful feature engineering, hyperparameter tuning, or domain-informed preprocessing.


## Learning Loop

Each challenge follows a five-step experiential learning loop:

1. **Inspect the AI agent's work.** Download the AI agent's code and reasoning trace from the platform. Study the agent's decisions: what preprocessing did it choose? What model architecture? What did it skip or get wrong?

2. **Attempt the challenge using AI as a tool.** Work in your local coding environment with AI coding assistants. Apply your own judgment to override the AI's decisions where you see weaknesses. You are encouraged to use any AI tools you like.

3. **Score comparison.** Submit your predictions on the AI Coding Gym platform. Your score will appear on the live leaderboard alongside the AI baseline and your peers.

4. **Work-in-progress sharing (in-class).** We will dedicate class time for 2-3 students to present their approaches (10 min each). Presentations are selected for methodological diversity, not top scores. Share what strategies you tried, what worked, and what didn't.

5. **Reflection write-up.** Write a structured reflection (see Deliverables below).


## Deliverables

### 1. Challenge Submission (via AI Coding Gym)
- Submit your predictions on the [AI Coding Gym](https://aicodinggym.com/) platform.
- You may submit as many times as you like. Your best score will be recorded.

### 2. Final Report (via Canvas)
Submit a **2-3 page report** (PDF) on Canvas containing:

- **AI Inspection Summary**: What did the AI agent do? What were its key decisions (model, preprocessing, features)? What weaknesses did you identify in its reasoning trace?

- **Your Strategy**: What did you change and why? Describe your approach, the prompts you used with AI assistants, and the domain judgment you applied that the AI did not.

- **Results Comparison**: Your score vs. the AI baseline. Include a screenshot of your leaderboard position.

- **Reflection** answering three questions:
  1. Where did the AI fall short?
  2. What did I do that the AI did not?
  3. What concept or skill did this teach me?


## Grading

| Component | Weight |
|-----------|--------|
| Challenge score (from AI Coding Gym leaderboard) | 30% |
| Reflection write-up (depth of analysis, not just reporting scores) | 40% |
| AI Diagnosis (identifying errors and weaknesses in the AI's approach) | 30% |

**Note:** A student who does not beat the AI baseline but writes a perceptive reflection correctly identifying why and what concept to strengthen earns a strong grade. Understanding matters more than raw score.
